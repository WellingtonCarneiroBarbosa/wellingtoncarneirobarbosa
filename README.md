### 👋 About

I started coding at 12, started building businesses at 16, and today I lead the engineering of a Brazilian payments company.

I'm the co-founder and CTO of **[Fruitfy](https://fruitfy.io)** — a digital marketplace and payment infrastructure for creators and sellers of digital products (courses, subscriptions, infoproducts). I architected and shipped the platform from the first commit as Tech Lead, and now lead a cross-functional team while staying hands-on with architecture, financial infrastructure, and code quality.

My work sits where **engineering meets financial regulation**: I spend as much time on ledger correctness, settlement flows and Banco Central compliance as I do on code review.

---

### 🏗️ What I'm building

**Fruitfy Intermediações e Pagamentos** — payment infrastructure for the Brazilian creator economy.

- **Pix rails end-to-end** — Pix In/Out, split payments, sub-acquiring for third-party gateways running on our infrastructure, configurable transaction limits and automated security controls.
- **Regulatory roadmap** — pursuing authorization as a **Payment Institution (IP)** regulated by Banco Central do Brasil. Target: **indirect Pix participant within 2 years, direct participant within 5**.
- **Risk & compliance engineering** — KYC/KYB onboarding with liveness verification, fraud detection on seller registration, admin review queues with SLA tracking, MED thresholds and rolling reserves.
- **Financial products** — receivables anticipation for sellers, and treasury strategies for idle platform balances.
- **Audit-grade observability** — logging and audit trails engineered for forensic examination: retention, integrity, hash chaining and digital evidence chain of custody.

### 📈 Traction

**In 8 months of active operation:**

- **~300k orders processed per month**, of which **80k–120k are approved transactions** (Aug 2026)

### 🧭 How I work

- **Financial correctness over speed.** In payments, a race condition isn't a bug — it's someone's money.
- **Regulation as an engineering requirement.** Compliance constraints get modeled into the system, not bolted on before an audit.
- **Hands-on leadership.** I still review PRs, design schemas and debug production. Architecture decisions shouldn't be made by people who don't run the system.
